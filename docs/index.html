Why did H33 score 0.2778 — and why this tip file can beat it?

H33's trick is coverage hygiene, not new discovery. It took a high-coverage Poisson-dotted parent (40,199 dots) and deleted every dot within 2 px of a mapped fault (→ 37,654 dots). At DTI's α=0.2 that saves ~500 of weighted false-positive mass where the collar could not cover hidden truth. Brilliant denominator trim, but it adds no new TP_w. The full algebra and 5 ranked next hypotheses are in the PhD analysis.

Why tips? Basin-and-Range normal faults grow by segment linkage under cover. The catalogue is mechanically truncated where fill buries the scarp; expert hidden faults include those blind continuations 0.3–0.7 km beyond the last mapped pixel and the relay ramps between subparallel strands. No prior site targets this population with explicit geometry — all use per-pixel edge/ridge detectors blind to flat cover. This file relocates 30% of H33 outward 3–7 px along the distance gradient (the tip-process zone) with 30° Riedel splay, keeping 70% backbone. At DTI 0.2778 any novel dot needs k>0.055 to pay; tip dots provide k=0.33–0.77 for co-linear truth within 2 px, so even a 1-in-4 hit yields +0.008–0.012. Three ranked, scoped hypotheses →
