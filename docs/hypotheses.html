Priority 1 · H86E-MMT · NOT_RUN_PROTOCOL_STOP

Cross-corridor GeoDAWN magnetic-marker translation with radiometric contact control

Specific layers
competition rtp (Band 2); competition tmi_hg (Band 3) and tmi_vg (Band 9), combined as analytical magnetic tilt theta_mag = atan2(tmi_vg, tmi_hg); competition tc (Band 6), pixel-verified as radiometric Total Count and used only as a surficial-contact control, not magnetic tilt
Physical signature
Test label-free, strike-windowed phase correlation of RTP and analytical magnetic-tilt marker profiles on opposite sides of candidate corridors. Require a consistent, nonzero lateral translation across adjacent strike windows, matched marker polarity/shape, and continuity through the corridor; compare against zero-shift and phase-scrambled controls. Use Band 6 Total Count only to flag radiometric/alluvial boundaries without a matching magnetic-marker offset. Estimate survey-seam directions from acquisition metadata rather than assuming a fixed azimuth. The magnetic products share acquisition/source ancestry and are not independent measurements.
Why an unmapped fault?
A blind transfer or accommodation fault may translate a buried volcanic marker beneath basin fill without producing a strong surface scarp. This is a mechanism to test, not an assertion that a detected magnetic offset is a fault or that a withheld catalogue segment represents a genuinely uncatalogued fault.
Different from this checkout
The reviewed GEMSDOE86 lanes principally score ridges, gradients, contacts, or local structural attributes; this hypothesis tests two-sided cross-corridor translation rather than a pointwise edge. The reviewed sibling metadata did not identify this exact phase-lag mechanism, but that search is not proof of global or competition-wide originality. The current shared feature cache and required raw feature TIFF are absent locally.
Named non-fault mimic
Intrusive contacts, primary volcanic-flow terminations, remanent-polarity changes, and GeoDAWN flight-line/tie-line leveling seams.
Qualitative DTI opportunity / implementation cost
Highest qualitative opportunity in this untested queue, with low confidence: displacement is more directly aligned to fault-trace prediction than a generic edge. No numeric HOLDOUT-DTI or leaderboard improvement is forecast. Medium-high; phase-correlation windows, acquisition-seam controls, matched controls, cache identity checks, and buffered whole-segment holdout. Runtime is unbenchmarked.

Signed paired-throw basement/gravity concordance augmented with Laplace gravity curvature

Specific layers
competition depth_to_base_surf (Band 15); competition iso_grav_anom (Band 13), iso_grav_anom_hg (Band 18), and iso_grav_anom_vg (Band 11); competition det_elev (Band 12), det_elev_slope (Band 19), and tmi_hg (Band 3)
Physical signature
Retain the signed cross-flank basement/gravity throw test, then add the footprint-masked, spatially regularized Laplace vertical gravity-curvature dipole g_zz = -(d_x Band18 - d_y Band11) as an additional geometric constraint along the same strike-consistent corridor. Compare with the standalone SPBT baseline and a polarity-scrambled control. Mask nodata before derivatives and do not treat Band 13, Bands 11/18, or their derived curvature as independent observations: they share gravity-data ancestry. The archived standalone SPBT implementation did not include this Laplace term.
Why an unmapped fault?
Hypothesis: alluvial/playa burial may mute surface slope while a blind intrabasin growth fault retains basement-depth and gravity flank structure; the curvature term could sharpen the cross-strike dipole geometry. Whole-segment holdout must test this before any claim about unmapped faults.
Different from this checkout
The standalone signed basement/gravity SPBT was already tested in H86E E1-E2 and falsified against its recorded polarity-scrambled and spaced-random controls. This is not a claim of a new standalone SPBT. The distinct proposed extension adds regularized Laplace gravity curvature; H86E E1-E2 did not implement or test that term. No reviewed GEMSDOE86 lane implemented this exact extension, but its incremental value over the negative base remains unknown.
Named non-fault mimic
Unfaulted paleovalley margins, buried caldera-moat density boundaries, gravity-inversion artifacts, and spatially correlated gravity-resampling noise.
Qualitative DTI opportunity / implementation cost
Low-to-moderate qualitative opportunity, ranked below MMT because the standalone base was negative and the added curvature is derived from the same gravity field. It remains a falsifiable, untested extension; no numeric gain is forecast. Medium-high; exact band masking, derivative regularization/sensitivity analysis, comparison against the negative base and polarity control, then buffered spatial holdout. Runtime is unbenchmarked.

Physical signature
Seek strike-continuous RTP troughs with positive cross-strike trough curvature and flanking shoulders, co-located with shallow conductivity and spatially aligned paleo-sinter/thermal anomalies in dilatant corridors. Compare against phase-scrambled and point-location controls; run a single-feature leakage canary for each external layer. A magnetic trough or geothermal deposit alone is not diagnostic of a fault.
Why an unmapped fault?

H86D-CTHB used positive magnetic-ridge and radial point-halo features. This proposal tests the sign-reversed trough/shoulder geometry plus pairwise spatial alignment, not a generic deposit halo. It excludes fault-compilation polygons and every distance-to-known-fault field. The screened local novelty review is limited to the cited repository/fleet scope.

Qualitative DTI opportunity / implementation cost
Moderate qualitative opportunity but low confidence: the physical association is relevant to concealed hydrothermal conduits, yet geothermal points are not fault labels and may be spatially biased. No numeric gain is forecast. Medium-high; licensed point cleaning, coordinate/grid checks, aligned-corridor construction, point-location controls, leakage canaries, and buffered holdout. Runtime is unbenchmarked.
