1 · Basement-curvature / gravity-curvature coherence

Mechanism: a blind fault or fault-controlled basin can deform the basement surface while juxtaposing density contrasts. Test scale-normalized, signed Hessian curvature and axial directions from basement depth against independently derived gravity curvature. Keep only preregistered multi-scale alignment/persistence; test sign rather than selecting it post hoc.

Novelty boundary: H86B already has basement step gradient/ridge, gravity tilt/gradient, and a magnetic–gravity contact-offset feature. The untried object is their explicit signed, orientation-consistent second-derivative cross-physics persistence, not “basement curvature” alone.

Why it could mark an unmapped fault: subsurface bending and density boundaries can persist beneath cover where surface traces are absent.

Cost/data: medium; the local owner-supplied mirror includes relevant layers, but the shared feature cache and independent source authentication are missing.

Validation: visible-only catalogue fields, pixel-exact visible-fault mask, feature-alone leakage canary, and the shared buffered whole-segment spatial holdout. No result exists.

2 · Windowed magnetic spectral source-depth transition

Mechanism: compute preregistered radial power-spectrum slopes over nested, overlapping windows of TMI/RTP, then test for persistent relative source-depth-regime boundaries that align with independent structure. This is a relative spectral proxy, not a direct Curie-depth or temperature measurement.

Novelty boundary: H86B already includes multi-scale magnetic ridges/Hessian, tilt/director disruption, and one 150 m upward-continuation attenuation gradient. It does not compute windowed spectral source-depth transitions.

Why it could matter: a depth-regime transition may distinguish shallow cover effects from deeper structures; its sign and relation to heat flow are not assumed.

Non-fault mimic: lithology/remanence, survey-leveling seams, altitude variation, and gridding artifacts.

Cost/data: high; TMI/RTP are represented in local mirrors, but a shared cache and authenticated source details are absent.

3 · Raw-earthquake magnitude-frequency and recurrence anomalies

Mechanism: from event-level locations, magnitudes, and times, estimate spatial Gutenberg–Richter b-value and recurrence/swarm residuals only after modeling local magnitude completeness. Do not assume a universal high/low b-value signature for geothermal faults.

Novelty boundary: an earlier H33 proposal considered epicenter density plus focal-plane orientation. This is different: magnitude-frequency and temporal recurrence conditioned on completeness, not event-density ridges or focal-plane lineaments. H86B itself uses no raw event catalog.

Non-fault mimic: quarry blasts, induced seismicity, aftershock sequences, network changes, and magnitude-completeness drift.

Cost/data: high and not viable in this checkout. The official USGS ComCat FDSN service is a source to inspect; no query was made and no event extract was restored.

4 · Depth-migrating MT conductivity boundary

Mechanism: if uncertainty-qualified MT inversion slices are available, trace whether a lateral conductivity/resistivity boundary migrates coherently with depth, consistent with a steep structure. Require inversion-resolution and uncertainty diagnostics before using the geometry.

Novelty boundary: H86B already uses a scalar surface conductivity layer and its gradient/ridge. A cross-depth boundary track requires a missing depth stack; it is not a renamed surface gradient.

Non-fault mimic: basin fill, lithologic contacts, inversion smoothing/regularization, and edge artifacts.

Cost/data: very high and currently unavailable in this checkout. The DOE GDR record 1391 is a manual-review link; its current availability and depth-slice contents were not verified.
