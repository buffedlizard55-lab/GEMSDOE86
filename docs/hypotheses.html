Detailed Geological Justifications

Rank 1: H86B-GFCO — Cover-Adaptive Geothermometer Disequilibrium & Contact-Offsets (Validated Top Candidate)

Specific Layers: GDR-1391 Geothermometry CSV (gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv: temp_c, geothermquartz_c, geothermchalc_c, geothermcat_c — with dist_known_fault_px strictly excluded), gdr_volcanic_vents_in_footprint.csv, training_features.tif (MT 500m–10km, TMI, TMI_up150m, TMI_RTP, CBA gravity, basement depth, geodetic strain, elevation, slope), 1 m LiDAR scarp features, and GeoDAWN radiometrics.

Physical Signature: High chemical geothermometer disequilibrium (ΔT = max(T_quartz, T_chalc, T_NaKCa) − T_meas ≥ 50°C) indicates rapid ascending convective flow along permeable fault conduits without conductive re-equilibration (Fournier & Potter, 1982; Giggenbach, 1988). Combined with the magnetic attenuation ratio (|∇TMI| / |∇TMI_up150m|) across basement steps (Grauch & Hudson, 2007) and strictly visible crack-tip & relay-ramp envelopes.

Why It Catches Missing Faults: Conventional catalogues are heavily biased toward visible bedrock scarps. This hypothesis directly images concealed hydrothermal upflow conduits and blind fault offsets beneath alluvial cover where surface topography is flat.

Difference from Prior Repo Work: Unlike PR #6 H86-RMRB (which relied on single-layer label-free radiometric residuals) or GEMSDOE73 (which lacked geothermometer disequilibrium and cover-blind dual LOQO heads), H86B-GFCO employs a 4-quadrant LOQO ensemble with cover-adaptive weighting and fracture-mechanics gating.

Holdout Validation Results:

  • Whole-Component 3-px Buffered LOQO (11,321 positives): 0.1078 [0.0908, 0.1254] vs H33-2-B2 = 0.0961 (+12.1%) and d2.8 = 0.0919 (+17.3%).
  • Segment-All LOQO (22,641 positives): 0.1396 [0.1286, 0.1513] vs d2.8 = 0.1011 (Δ = +0.0385 [+0.0269, +0.0524], +38.1%).
  • Segment-Detached LOQO (22,619 positives): 0.1521 [0.1378, 0.1662] vs d2.8 = 0.1035 (Δ = +0.0486 [+0.0343, +0.0627], +47.0%).

Rank 2: H86C-TDPL — Topographically-Deconfounded 1 m LiDAR Piedmont Scarplets

Specific Layers: lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif (step_max, downfacing, upfacing, relief, coherence) + det_elev / det_slope.

Physical Signature: Multi-scale (5, 9, 17 px) steerable Radon collinearity tensor coupled with contour-orthogonality suppression: rejects horizontal benches parallel to topographic contours (|∇z · t_scarp| ≈ 0) to eliminate pluvial Lake Lahontan shoreline terraces while enhancing cross-contour tectonic scarps.

Why It Catches Missing Faults: Subtle 0.3–1.5 m Holocene fault scarps in piedmont alluvium are completely invisible in 100 m DEM grids but appear as sharp, linear phase-congruence steps in 1 m LiDAR.

Rank 3: H86D-PFCO — Paired Potential-Field Ridge Dislocation Detector

Specific Layers: GeoDAWN TMI_RTP, TMI_up150m, CBA Bouguer gravity (training_features.tif bands 1–4, 21, 22).

Physical Signature: Extraction of 1D magnetic phase-symmetry ridges followed by graph matching of truncated conjugate ridge tips across 300 m–2.5 km lateral offsets, scoring transverse bridge likelihood where horizontal gravity gradient peaks.

Why It Catches Missing Faults: Unmapped cross-faults in sedimentary basins displace older volcanic/plutonic units. The fault itself may have low magnetization, but the lateral offset between conjugate marker beds definitively pinpoints the cutting fault trace.

Rank 4: H86E-MTDA — 3D Magnetotelluric Dipping-Conductor Vector

Specific Layers: INGENIOUS 3D MT resistivity inversion depth slices (500 m, 1 km, 2 km, 4 km, 6 km, 10 km).

Physical Signature: Tracking the lateral migration vector &vec;v_z = argmax |∇ log ρ(z)| across consecutive depth slices to establish the 3D strike and 50°–65° normal dip of conductive smectite/illite alteration zones, projecting the plane updip to the surface.

Why It Catches Missing Faults: Normal faults dip at ~60°. A deep conductive geothermal reservoir at 3 km depth sits ~1.7 km laterally down-dip from its surface fault trace. Projecting deep MT gradients updip locates blind faults that have no surface expression.

Rank 5: H86F-RLSL — Relocated Microseismicity Planar Clustering

Specific Layers: USGS ComCat FDSN API + UNR Nevada Seismological Laboratory catalog (M ≥ 1.0, 1980–2026).

Physical Signature: 3D RANSAC planar fitting of hypocenters with daytime quarry-blast filtering, projected updip along focal mechanism nodal planes.

Why It Catches Missing Faults: Active fault strands produce micro-earthquakes at 2–10 km depth, revealing seismogenic structures that have not ruptured the surface in recorded history.
