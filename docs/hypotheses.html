Priority 1 · H86C-MMT · NOT_RUN_PROTOCOL_STOP

Cross-fault magnetic marker translation

Specific layers
competition rtp; competition tmi
Physical signature
For label-free candidate line orientations, phase-correlate magnetic texture on opposite flanks. Require an along-line translation of transverse magnetic markers, consistent in adjacent windows, and a weaker zero-shift match. Keep both signed slip directions rather than assuming one regional sense of slip.
Why an unmapped fault?
A buried shear zone may truncate and displace a pre-existing magnetic marker even without a mapped surface trace. Searching the image rather than extrapolating catalogue tips makes this capable in principle of finding off-catalogue structures.
Different from this checkout
GFCO compares TMI with upward-continued TMI and constructs visible-tip/relay priors; GMC compares gradients between different physical fields. Neither estimates along-fault displacement of transverse markers in two flanks of the same magnetic image. The concurrently merged WVB lane uses local-wavenumber/depth concordance and fault-valve coupling, not flank-to-flank along-fault marker translation.
Named non-fault mimic
Unfaulted intrusive contacts with unrelated texture; acquisition-block seams and remanent magnetization changes.
Expected value / implementation cost
Highest qualitative priority, very uncertain; no measured gain or score projection. Medium-high: oriented flank extraction, bounded phase correlation, uncertainty and seam controls.
Data / viability
NOT_READY: the raw mirrored core can be restored, but the required shared cached stack is not present. Do not rebuild a substitute under this protocol. No new external product is proposed as acquired.

Priority 2 · H86-MBGR · NOT_RUN_PROTOCOL_AND_PROVENANCE_STOP

Basement–gravity forward-residual displacement

Specific layers
competition iso_grav_anom (band 13); competition depth_to_base_surf (band 15)
Physical signature
Fit a spatially cross-fitted, low-frequency forward gravity response of basement depth using only training-region rock-density priors. Test line-like *residual* changes in its signed transverse gradient that persist when the physical model is refitted without the test block; compare against unmodeled raw gravity edges and same-sign paired steps.
Why an unmapped fault?
A buried displaced interface can leave a localized gravity misfit even when an exposed scarp or mapped surface trace is absent; a catalogue-independent residual test can search covered basins rather than extrapolating known tips.
Different from this checkout
H86-RMRB regressed radiometric K~Th+U against RTP, not a gravity forward-response residual. PR #60 SPT intersected raw signed basement/gravity flank steps using the full-catalogue collar; this instead asks whether a cross-fitted physical forward model fails locally and does not reuse its trial as a novel method.
Named non-fault mimic
Variation in basin-fill density, inverse-model artifacts and igneous contacts; if the basement depth was derived from the same gravity measurements, the residual is circular and cannot be interpreted as independent confirmation.
Expected value / implementation cost
Second qualitative information opportunity if independent provenance is confirmed; no numeric forecast or holdout score. Medium-high: verify inversion lineage and units, physically constrained forward model, spatial refits, residual/seam and density-null controls.
Data / viability
NOT_READY: must establish whether basement depth was inverted from this gravity band and restore actual shared cached features; if input independence is impossible, retire before testing. No new external product is proposed as acquired.

Priority 3 · H86C-WCS · NOT_RUN_MISSING_DATA_AND_PROTOCOL_STOP

Watershed-crossing scarp persistence

Specific layers
official USGS 3DEP bare-earth 1 m DEM; competition det_elev for regional detrending
Physical signature
Measure signed scarp steps that retain strike and down-side polarity across independently delineated drainage divides. Reject candidates that only follow a channel, constant-elevation shoreline, road bench, or one alluvial fan boundary.
Why an unmapped fault?
A small tectonic scarp may be missed in regional mapping; persistence across unrelated catchments is evidence beyond a single ridge or lineament.
Different from this checkout
GFCO uses scarplet collinearity, piedmont hinges, valley chains and relief deconfounding. It does not condition the signed step on independent watershed topology across divides. WVB uses relief-normalized scarps and shoreline controls, not independently delineated watershed topology.
Named non-fault mimic
Paleolake shorelines, fluvial terraces and road cuts. Drainage divides alone cannot distinguish these from a fault.
Expected value / implementation cost
Third qualitative priority; physically useful discriminant but expensive and geographically incomplete until DEM coverage is verified. No score projection. High: native-resolution DEM acquisition, hydrologic conditioning, catchments and raster aggregation.
Data / viability
NOT_VIABLE_YET: no native DEM bytes, license-specific product manifest, complete coverage audit, or shared cached watershed stack. Not offered as a runnable experiment. Official metadata and the competition description were retrieved through the page-fetch service on 2026-10-23. Neither the authenticated CSV nor the required native DEM tiles were acquired here. Metadata reachability is not data obtainability.

Validation plan after blockers are resolved

  1. Assign one method lane; restore and hash-verify the actual shared cached feature stack and a current complete accessible registry.
  2. Preserve the literal rho and overlap limits; do not exempt a dense prior without an explicitly revised protocol.

  6. Do not compare maxima from different fold designs as a current holdout best. A positive paired result and all uniqueness gates are required before separate slot selection.
  7. At most three experiments or two hours; a negative result is a deliverable. No organizer submission is authorized by this plan.
