1.1 Mathematical Properties of the Distance-Tolerant Tversky Index (DTI)

The competition evaluates predictions using the Distance-Tolerant Tversky Index with parameters α = 0.2, β = 0.8, and a 300 m (3 pixels at 100 m/px) triangular distance-decay kernel:

DTI = TPtol / (TPtol + 0.2 · FPtol + 0.8 · FNtol)

where the triangular tolerance kernel for distance d to true fault is:

k(d) = max(0, 1 - d / 300 m)

While the low α = 0.2 penalty for false positives appears to reward dense predictions, continuous connected lineaments (such as the unthinned H19-5 backbone with 121,131 pixels) incur massive false-positive penalties. When multiple adjacent pixels sit within the 300 m radius of a true fault, only the maximum kernel value contributes to true-positive credit (diminishing marginal returns), while every additional prediction pixel contributes 0.2 × (1 - k(d)) to the denominator penalty.

1.2 Spacing Optimization: Priority Disc Thinning (d2.8)

By thinning the prediction surface to a minimum nearest-neighbor distance of r = 2.8 px (≥ √8 ≈ 2.828 px, or ~283 m), adjacent dots have non-overlapping triangular tolerance cones. This reduced the active prediction count from 121,131 to 44,090 dots—a 63.6% reduction in false-positive denominator mass—while preserving over 90% of tolerance cone coverage.

1.3 Catalogue Flank Pruning (d_cat > 2.0 px)

In the official competition protocol, known mapped faults (existing_faults.tif) are excluded from the private evaluation set. Predictions placed in the immediate digitizing halo (d ≤ 2 px) of mapped faults cannot earn true-positive credit on genuinely unmapped faults, but incur full 0.2 × FP penalty.
