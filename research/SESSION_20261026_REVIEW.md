### 1.2 Why the subset scored highest (mechanism, with causal caution)

The competition metric is the asymmetric DTI (distance-Tversky, α = 0.2,
β = 0.8, 300 m triangular kernel): truth-side credit takes a **max** over each
3-px disk (saturating), while false-positive mass **sums** every emitted pixel.
For a non-redundant dot, adding it improves DTI only if its marginal kernel
credit exceeds roughly α·DTI/(1 − β·DTI)·(cost scale) — at DTI ≈ 0.26 the bar
is ≈ 0.07 truth-credit per unit FP mass (repository derivation in earlier
reviews; see `research/H33_INTERPRETATION_REVIEW.md` for counterexamples).
Dots in the 1–2 px collar of an already-mapped fault are the most likely to be
redundant (their disks mostly re-cover ground the catalogue-adjacent ridge
already credits, and the hidden test faults are by construction *not* the
mapped ones). The owner-reported sequence 44,090 → 0.2600, 40,199 → 0.2708,
37,654 (collar-pruned) → 0.2778, then +1,200 dots → 0.2750 is exactly the
signature of pruning below-bar mass and then re-adding some. The denser
1.5-px-spaced fields (GEMSDOE24/27, ~60k dots, 0.2449–0.2477) sit lower,
consistent with FP mass summing while TP credit saturates.

**Causal caution (why this is not a proof of geology):** all nine scores are
`OWNER-REPORTED` with no organizer receipts, submission dates, or split
information in-repo; the prompt itself cites two different "current
leaderboard highs" (0.4413 and 0.3195) — logged as
`IR-86-5620-LEADERBOARD-CONFLICT`, unresolved. The subset trend is strong
evidence that *those particular collar dots* had below-bar marginal credit on
*that* hidden set; it does not identify a transferable geological mechanism,
and the same pruning applied to a different dot field need not help (several
in-repo counterexamples exist). Lineage is proven; the score ordering is a
single unverified association.

### 1.3 Can we generate one scoring > 0.2778?

No honest path is demonstrated. Arithmetic: +0.01 DTI at ~37.7k dots requires
on the order of 400 additional kernel-credit units — i.e. hundreds of dots
landing within 300 m of *currently unmapped* faults that the H19-5/H33 ridge
network misses, without adding offsetting FP mass. Rearranging the same ridge
network cannot supply that (and is a protocol duplicate anyway). Two
instruments available in-repo both warn against extrapolation:

- `HOLDOUT-DTI` hide-and-recover measures recovery of *catalogue-like* faults.
  The fold-fair H33 analogue scores **0.0036** there (this session, and
  consistent with prior lanes) while owner-reports 0.2778 on the portal — the
  local instrument and the hidden test reward different things (the hidden set
  is enriched for faults the catalogue missed).
- `SGMC-PROXY-DTI` (off-catalogue USGS bedrock faults) is the closer analogue
  and is where this session's candidate also failed (§3).

Any future claim of "> 0.2778" requires an organizer receipt. This session
produces none and authorizes no slot to test one.
