## Why did `h33-h33-2-b2` (0.2778 OWNER-REPORTED) lead the fleet, and can it be beaten?

Verified file facts (re-measured this session from `data/scored_fleet/`): 37,654 binary
dots, 0 on-catalogue, 0 within 2 px of the catalogue, mean distance to catalogue
**29.0 px (2.9 km)**, all-finite float32 in [0,1]. It is the H19-5 multiline-corroborated
ridge ancestor (121,131 px; 0.1922 OWNER-REPORTED), Poisson-disk dotted at 2.8 px
(44,090 dots; 0.2600), then pruned of every dot ≤2 px from the mapped catalogue (B2).

Mechanism, in order of contribution (metric algebra: alpha 0.2, beta 0.8, 300 m
triangular kernel; a dot is profitable iff its realised kernel credit exceeds
≈ alpha·DTI ≈ 0.056):

1. **The ancestor surface is the asset.** Every fleet file above 0.26 OWNER-REPORTED is
   this one H19-5/D2.8 lineage (92.6–100% 3-px overlap with R0, measured earlier and
   reconfirmed in this session's forensic table, `.cache/fleet_forensic.json` values
   reproduced in the PR description). H19-5 combined topographic openness, magnetic/
   gravity gradient ridges and thermal-spring proximity — it mimics how the organizer's
   experts themselves interpret GeoDAWN + DEM lineaments (official problem page:
   test faults were manually identified by fault experts, GeoDAWN region chunked
   into public/private sets).
2. **Dotting converts lines into coverage.** Truth credit saturates at the kernel max,
   so a solid 1-px ridge pays false-positive mass for coverage one dot already bought.
   The 0.1922 → 0.2600 jump is pure emission geometry at fixed surface.
3. **B2 pruning removes structurally starved mass.** Dots 1–2 px from a mapped trace
   spend most of their 300 m kernel on already-known fault that cannot earn credit in
   the initial round, while paying near-full FP. The 0.2600 → 0.2778 step deletes
   exactly that mass (with the documented caveat that a collar dot *can* still recover
   a hidden splay; see `research/H33_INTERPRETATION_REVIEW.md`).
4. **Distance-to-catalogue distribution is the strongest single fleet correlate.**
   Fresh this session (15 scored-fleet files with OWNER-REPORTED scores): Spearman
   between mean dot distance-to-catalogue and owner-reported score is **+0.78**
   (+0.61 excluding the R0 ancestor family, n=12). The worst file in the fleet
   (`gems81-cont`, 0.0041) has mean distance 2.6 px; every file above 0.23 sits at
   17–29 px. Association, not controlled causation — good surfaces also spread —
   but it is consistent with the official design: the test population is
   *regionally chunked expert mapping*, not catalogue splays.

Can 0.2778 be beaten? In principle yes, and the reported 0.4413 leaderboard ceiling
(conflicting with 0.3195 elsewhere in the prompt; both OWNER-REPORTED, unresolved)
implies somebody's surface recovers far more of the expert population. The algebra
says DTI 0.4413 needs weighted recall ≳ 0.39 at zero FP. Emission rearrangement of
the H19-5 ancestor is exhausted (53 variants, |Δ| ≤ 0.0073 on surrogates, PRs #50–55).
What is required is a better *surface for the expert-mapped population* — most
plausibly native-resolution LiDAR scarp evidence and/or a supervised model whose
feature planes encode expert-visible lineaments *without* collapsing into a
catalogue halo. This session tested two such ideas honestly; neither qualifies yet.

## Ranked hypotheses considered (layers · signature · why unmapped · novelty)

1. **H86E-SPBT — signed paired-throw basement/gravity concordance** (preregistered
   rank 1 in `registry/h86c_hypotheses.json`). Layers: `depth_to_base_surf` (15),
   `iso_grav_anom` (13), `det_elev`/`det_elev_slope` (12/19), `tmi_hg` (3). Signature:
   antisymmetric ±200–400 m flank steps with opposite signs (deeper basement =
   gravity low on the downthrown side), ≥900 m strike continuity, muted-relief weight.
   Why unmapped: intrabasin blind growth faults under Holocene fill keep the basement/
   gravity step after the scarp is buried. Novelty: all prior lanes used unsigned
   gradient magnitudes; none tested signed cross-flank polarity concordance.
   Mimic: paleovalley / caldera-moat margins. **RUN (E1–E2): falsified.**
2. **H86E-CCTX — visible-catalogue-context supervised LOQO.** Layers: all 19
   competition bands + gradients + LiDAR/topo/radiometric externals + SPBT channel +
   other-component catalogue distance/density context. Signature: learned fault
   likelihood; catalogue context was deliberately excluded by GFCO/KSLB
   (`catalogue_used: false`), and distance-to-visible alone has canary AUC 0.755.
   Why unmapped: splays, tip extensions and relay links of the mapped network plus
   fault-like geophysics away from it. Mimic: lithologic contacts / mapped-network
   halo. **RUN (E3): strong holdout, failed promotion gates.**
3. **H86E-MMT — magnetic marker-horizon phase translation** (`rtp` 2, `tc` 6,
   `tmi_hg` 3, radiometrics): two-sided flank phase correlation detecting lateral
   offset of buried volcanic markers across transfer faults. Not run (budget).
4. **H86E-HMDC — hydrothermal magnetite-destruction troughs + vent-alignment
   corridors** (`rtp`, `tmi_vg` 9, `cond_surf` 17, dilatation 8; GDR 1391 sinter/
   2 m-probe/vent points). Not run (budget).
5. **H86E-WCS — watershed-crossing 1 m LiDAR micro-scarps** (USGS 3DEP tiles via the
   competition DEM-links CSV; free/official, obtainable, but native-tile processing
   exceeds this session's remaining budget). Not run.

Ranking basis: expected information for the catalogue-missing population per
implementation cost. SPBT was cheapest and most orthogonal; CCTX had the strongest
single-feature canary behind it.
