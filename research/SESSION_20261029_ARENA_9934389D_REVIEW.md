## What H33's reported 0.2778 does—and does not—show

The best-supported description is a **plausible metric mechanism, not a causal explanation**. H33 is a 37,654-dot member of the same H27-4/D28 lineage as the owner-reported 0.2708 parent. The recorded pixel comparison removes 2,545 near-catalogue dots in the `1 < d_cat <= 2` pixel collar while retaining the far-field component; the outside-footprint encoding also differs. The arithmetic difference, `+0.0070`, is a difference between two **OWNER-REPORTED** claims—not a paired experiment.

Under the 300 m triangular distance-weighted Tversky objective (`alpha=0.2` FP weight, `beta=0.8` FN weight), a dot near an already-covered catalogue trace can add false-positive cost while contributing little new truth coverage. Removing some redundant near-trace dots could therefore help *if* their marginal truth credit were small. But the withheld organizer truth is unavailable here; those same dots could have earned nearby credit, and encoding changes or other pipeline differences could matter. Cross-site differences cannot isolate this mechanism. We cannot conclude that collar pruning caused the reported increase, that the reported increase is reproducible, or that it predicts a new submission's score.

A separate historical comparator is **HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0961 [0.0828, 0.1083]** (`gems57-pooled-hide-v2`, **11,321 withheld positives**, buffered whole-component LOQO; archived fold-fair H33-2-B2 control in [`docs/evidence/h86b-gfco-run-card.json`](../docs/evidence/h86b-gfco-run-card.json)).
