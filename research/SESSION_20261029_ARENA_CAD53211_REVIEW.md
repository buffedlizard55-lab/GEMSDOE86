## Why H33 may have ranked highest; what the evidence does and does not show

GEMSDOE32 H33’s reported `0.2778` is the highest **supplied owner claim among these nine**. It is not proven to be the current official leaderboard leader, and it is not organizer-confirmed. The user-supplied `0.3195` and `0.4413` leaderboard claims conflict and were not verified. The DrivenData portal/leaderboard was not scraped; this repository contains no submission-page receipt.

The most specific measured explanation is a **nested, deletion-only change**, not a new geological discovery: the GEMSDOE28 H27-4 parent has 40,199 positive dots; H33 has 37,654, removing 2,545 points in the `1 < d_cat <= 2 px` catalogue collar and adding none. In the repository’s 300 m triangular DTI/Tversky implementation (`alpha = 0.2`, `beta = 0.8`), reducing predicted mass can lower the false-positive contribution while retaining broad coverage. The direction of the weights makes that a plausible mechanism, not a guarantee: deleted dots could still receive credit through the score kernel for withheld positives. The parent and child are not a preregistered, isolated causal test; their score reports are owner claims, and the outside-footprint encodings differ. A `-zeros` export is a format treatment, not an evidenced score treatment.

An archived internal H33-like result is **HOLDOUT-DTI** (`gems57-pooled-hide-v2`, 11,321 withheld positive occurrences): `0.0961`, 95% CI `[0.0830, 0.1089]`. This is internal hide-and-recover evidence on its own split, not a leaderboard estimate, not proof that the exact submitted bytes earned 0.2778, and not directly comparable to the organizer score. A separate later fold-fair H33 control in the H86D analysis is **HOLDOUT-DTI 0.1056 [0.0931, 0.1187]** on a different 22,893-withheld-positive setup; its paired delta interval `[-0.0101, +0.0241]` crosses zero. 1. **Pleistocene Lake Lahontan shoreline displacement — rank 1, conditional moderate potential, high cost.** Layers: official USGS 3DEP raw 1 m bare-earth DEM, same-level/age-tagged Lahontan shoreline geometry from USGS NGMDB, native Band 12 `det_elev` and Band 19 `det_elev_slope`, with Band 15/13 only as independent context. Signature: a repeated, strike-coherent step/tilt of the same-age shoreline bench across adjacent segments, not a generic elevation edge. Rationale: an old concealed fault could displace a formerly near-level shoreline without a sharp modern scarp. Difference from existing work: shoreline margins were screened mainly as mimics; this proposal uses a dated shoreline as a positive displacement datum. Mimics include wave-cut benches, age/level miscorrelation, differential compaction, terrace risers and volcanic-flow margins. Source links: [USGS 3DEP](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program), [USGS NGMDB](https://ngmdb.usgs.gov/). 2. **Time-ordered ComCat sequence migration — rank 2, low-to-moderate/highly uncertain potential, very high cost.** Layers: quality-tagged USGS ComCat event time, hypocenter, magnitude and uncertainty; native Bands 7/8 as spatial context, optional Bands 13/17 only after band identity/fold handling is validated. Signature: repeated, quality-filtered events migrating along a fitted buried plane with stable direction/depth behavior under completeness thresholds. Rationale: a concealed active structure may generate temporal migration smeared out by static event-density layers. Difference: repository features use static counts/densities; this tests event order and sequence direction (not generic spatial seismic clustering). Mimics: injection/production swarms, blasts/quarries, volcanic/dike swarms, aftershocks on mapped faults, and network-detection changes. Signature: multiple age/lithology-matched contacts terminating or stepping consistently across a corridor, with map uncertainty and a corroborating subsurface break. Rationale: concealed displacement can offset bedrock contacts where a Quaternary surface-fault trace was not digitized. Difference: current SGMC is a fault-proxy raster; inspected methods do not build an age-aware contact-correspondence graph. Mimics: primary igneous contacts, depositional facies, alluvial cover boundaries, map-sheet seams, generalization, age-correlation errors and georeferencing.
