## Scientific interpretation

### What the H33 construction can and cannot explain

The owner-reported H33 competition result has no organizer submission-page receipt here. The available GEMSDOE32 page describes kernel-scale thinning and removal of predictions near the mapped catalogue, labels its estimate as a projection and includes `UNSCORED`. An older unscored snapshot does not disprove a later owner-obtained result. The mutually different claimed current highs in the request are retained as unverified historical claims, not silently reconciled into a current leaderboard observation.

The plausible mechanism is **coverage efficiency**, not a newly discovered geothermal layer. In the official maximum triangular-kernel metric, extra prediction mass can be redundant for truth recovery while increasing false-positive mass. But removing a dot can delete the only cover for a parallel or branching unmapped fault; the official public description does not establish that every two-pixel catalogue collar is excluded from private truth.

Write weighted recovered truth as `T`, false-positive mass as `F`, and fixed positive mass as `N`. With the shared implementation's epsilon, `DTI = T / (0.2*T + 0.2*F + 0.8*N + epsilon)`. If pruning loses truth credit `a` and removes false-positive mass `b`, cross multiplication gives the exact improvement condition `T*b > a*(F + 4*N + 5*epsilon)` when both denominators are positive. The existing randomized arithmetic regression checks this against the pinned shared metric. This is not a score projection, proof of an optimal spacing or a causal explanation of a private result.

No verified improvement over the owner's competition result is established in this review. Recovering deliberately withheld catalogue faults remains a distribution-shifted proxy for genuinely uncatalogued expert-mapped faults. Fault probability is also not evidence of sufficient geothermal heat, open permeability, fluid availability or an economic resource.

### Hypotheses declared before implementation

The three ranked ideas in [`registry/h86c_hypotheses.json`](../registry/h86c_hypotheses.json) are qualitative plans, not an assigned lane, numerical gain forecasts or runnable candidates:

1. **Cross-fault magnetic marker translation:** phase-correlate transverse magnetic markers on opposite flanks of label-free candidate lines, requiring a stable along-line translation rather than another gradient intersection. Intrusive contacts, remanent magnetization and acquisition seams are named mimics. The Bay Area concealed-fault magnetic-offset report motivates the mechanism, not transfer performance in the Great Basin. The exact shared magnetic stack and seam/coverage metadata are absent.
2. **Signed paired-throw consistency:** basement, gravity and detrended-relief profiles must have mechanically compatible side polarity. An erosional basement escarpment, lateral density change or inversion artifact may give the same signature; buried offsets may also be old and inactive. Depth sign, datum, bandwidth and inversion uncertainty require verification before implementation.
3. **Watershed-crossing scarp persistence:** signed native-DEM steps persisting across independent catchments, with shoreline/terrace/road controls. Native 3DEP coverage and product bytes were not acquired, so this is explicitly **NOT_VIABLE_YET**, not a resampled 100 m DEM promised as a 1 m product.
