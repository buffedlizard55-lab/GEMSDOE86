### 1.1 Exact Mathematical Mechanics of the Pooled 300 m Distance-Weighted Tversky Index
The competition metric (`vendor/template66/gems57/evaluate_holdout.py`, matching official reference `INGENIOUS_libs/utils.py::DifferentiableTverskyLoss` with $\alpha = 0.2$, $\beta = 0.8$, 100 m pixels, and isotropic 300 m triangular kernel $K(\Delta r, \Delta c) = \max(0, 1 - \sqrt{\Delta r^2 + \Delta c^2}/3)$) evaluates a predicted raster $P \in [0, 1]^{H \times W}$ against binary unmapped truth $G \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$ inside the competition footprint $\Omega \setminus M_{\text{cat}}$ ($M_{\text{cat}}$ = mapped `existing_faults.tif`):

$$\widetilde{P}(x) = \max_{y:\,\|x-y\|_2 < 3} P(y)\,K(x-y), \qquad \widetilde{G}(x) = \max_{y:\,\|x-y\|_2 < 3} G(y)\,K(x-y)$$

$$T(P) = \sum_{x \in \Omega \setminus M_{\text{cat}}} G(x)\,\widetilde{P}(x), \qquad F(P) = \sum_{x \in \Omega \setminus M_{\text{cat}}} P(x)\bigl(1 - \widetilde{G}(x)\bigr), \qquad N = \sum_{x \in \Omega \setminus M_{\text{cat}}} G(x)$$

$$\mathrm{DTI}(P) = \frac{T(P)}{T(P) + 0.2\,F(P) + 0.8\,\bigl(N - T(P)\bigr) + \varepsilon} = \frac{T(P)}{0.2\,T(P) + 0.2\,F(P) + 0.8\,N + \varepsilon}$$

Three structural consequences govern every fleet score in `ledger/prior_scores.csv`:

1. **Supremum credit vs. additive false-positive penalty (why binary Poisson discs crush continuous fields):**
   Because $\widetilde{P}(x)$ uses a **pointwise $\max$** over the 29-pixel 300 m disk whereas $F(P)$ **sums** $P(y)(1 - \widetilde{G}(y))$ over every positive pixel, placing continuous probability halos or 1-pixel-adjacent ridge lines pays linear false-positive mass in the denominator without increasing $\max_{y} P(y) K(x-y)$ once one unit dot ($P(y)=1$) sits within $1\text{–}2\text{ px}$ of a hidden fault trace. A single isolated unit dot contributes at most $1.0$ pixel of false-positive mass ($0.2$ denominator units) when placed completely outside the 300 m halo of hidden truth, whereas a $3\times 3$ patch of probability $0.5$ contributes $4.5$ mass ($0.9$ denominator units) for *half* the peak kernel credit ($0.5$ vs $1.0$). This explains why continuous rasters (`GEMSDOE3`: `0.0680`, `GEMSDOE16`: `0.1578` `OWNER-REPORTED`) and dense ridge/skeleton rasters (`GEMSDOE51` 415k px: `0.0725`, `GEMSDOE62` 151k px: `0.1407` `OWNER-REPORTED`) systematically underperform sparse $r \approx 2.8\text{ px}$ unit-dot packings (`GEMSDOE20`–`GEMSDOE36`: `0.2647`–`0.2778` `OWNER-REPORTED`).
2. **Exact pixel forensics of `GEMSDOE32` (`0.2778` `OWNER-REPORTED`) vs `GEMSDOE28` (`0.2708` `OWNER-REPORTED`):**
   Byte-exact diffing of the pinned `GEMSDOE28` (`40,199` unit dots) and `GEMSDOE32` (`h33-h33-2-b2`, `sha256=c55bafc4...`, `37,654` unit dots) rasters across all `12,279,160` grid cells proves **zero differing pixels outside the $1.0 < d_{\text{cat}} \le 2.0\text{ px}$ (100–200 m) mapped-fault collar**: `GEMSDOE32` was obtained solely by deleting the `2,545` `GEMSDOE28` dots lying within 100–200 m of `existing_faults.tif`. Removing prediction mass $\Delta F = b$ that loses truth credit $\Delta T = a$ strictly increases DTI whenever:
   $$T \cdot b > a \cdot (F + 4N + 5\varepsilon)$$
   Geologically and cartographically, the USGS/NBMG Quaternary + INGENIOUS structural catalogue already digitizes the principal slip surface of mapped faults; lateral digitization jitter ($<100\text{ m}$) is absorbed inside $d_{\text{cat}} \le 1\text{ px}$, whereas genuinely unmapped blind/stepover/splay faults branch away from mapped traces at en-echelon relay ramps, horse-tail/wing-crack tips ($d_{\text{cat}} \sim 300\text{ m–}4\text{ km}$), or intra-graben accommodation zones.
