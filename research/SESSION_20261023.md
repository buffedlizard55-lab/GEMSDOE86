## Why H33 might help, and what is not known

The [pinned H33 audit](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE32/blob/780a5a534269c79adfe6d1d7d5aadead161332aa/docs/downloads/gemsdoe32-h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-audit.json)
describes pruning dots near mapped faults from an earlier field. It contains explicit
`UNSCORED` wording and a `predicted_leaderboard_dti` field, not an authenticated portal
receipt. This does **not** prove the owner's later reported result never occurred. It
means the supplied historical scalar is unverified by the available receipt evidence.
Neither a causal attribution nor a claim to exceed it is justified.

For a distance-weighted Tversky index, let T be recovered truth mass, F false-positive mass
and N the fixed truth count. The shared formula is
`T / (0.2*T + 0.2*F + 0.8*N + epsilon)`.
Pruning that loses truth coverage a and removes false-positive mass b improves the index
exactly when `T*b > a*(F + 4*N + 5*epsilon)` (positive denominators). A redundant dot can
add false-positive cost without increasing maximum kernel coverage, which explains the
**possibility**, not a guarantee, of improvement from thinning or collar pruning. A true
unmapped parallel strand can instead lose valuable coverage. This arithmetic identity is
unit-tested against the shared metric; it is not a geological discovery or a score.

Fault recovery is also not vent detection. Faults can conduct geothermal fluid, but
lithologic contacts, dry faults and inversion artifacts can resemble these signals.
Catalogue hide-and-recover cannot by itself establish generalization to expert-mapped
previously unknown faults, much less the existence of a geothermal resource.
