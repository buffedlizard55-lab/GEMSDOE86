### 1.3 Exact Mathematical Mechanism Under the Official Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (DTI)
In `vendor/gems57/metric.py`, the official-form Distance-weighted Tversky Index with $\alpha=0.2$, $\beta=0.8$, and $300\text{ m}$ ($R=3\text{ px}$) triangular distance kernel $K(\delta) = \max(0, 1 - \|\delta\|_2 / 3)$ is:
$$\mathrm{DTI}(P, G) = \frac{T}{0.2\,T + 0.2\,F + 0.8\,|G| + \varepsilon}$$
where:
- **Truth recovery (`tpw`):** $T(P, G) = \sum_{g \in G} \max_{\|j - g\|_2 \le 3} P_j K(j - g)$ uses a **pointwise supremum (`max`)** over the $3\text{ px}$ disk around each hidden truth pixel $g \in G$.
- **False-positive mass (`fpw`):** $F(P, G) = \sum_{j \in \Omega} P_j \left(1 - \max_{\|g - j\|_2 \le 3} G_g K(j - g)\right)$ sums **linearly** over every predicted pixel $j$.

This max-vs-sum asymmetry governs the entire fleet's score distribution through three exact theorems:

1. **Why binary unit dots ($P_j \in \{0, 1\}$) with Euclidean disc spacing $r \approx 2.8\text{ px}$ beat continuous probability rasters (`0.04–0.21` $\to$ `0.2665`):**
   - A continuous probability wall of width $w$ pixels around a predicted trace contributes only its peak value $\max P_j \le 1$ to $T$, while paying linear false-positive penalty $\sum P_j$ across every off-fault pixel in the wall.
   - Concentrating mass into unit dots ($P_j = 1$) spaced at $r = 2.8\text{ px} = \sqrt{8}\text{ px}$ guarantees that every pixel along a continuous predicted strand lies within $\le 1.4\text{ px}$ of a unit dot (providing $K \ge 1 - 1.4/3 = 0.533$ truth credit, and $K=1.0$ within $3\text{ px}$ perpendicular capture) while cutting total predicted mass $\sum P_j$ by an order of magnitude ($\sim 37.6\text{k} - 44.1\text{k}$ dots vs $>300\text{k}$ continuous pixels).

     $$\mathrm{DTI}(S \setminus \Delta S) > \mathrm{DTI}(S) \iff \frac{T - a}{0.2(T - a) + 0.2(F - b) + 0.8|G| + \varepsilon} > \frac{T}{0.2T + 0.2F + 0.8|G| + \varepsilon}$$
