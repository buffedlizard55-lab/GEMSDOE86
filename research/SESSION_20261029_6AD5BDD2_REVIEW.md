## Repository-screened geological hypotheses (all unrun and unauthorized)

The existing register contains three fully specified, repository-screened hypotheses and explicitly excludes repeated concepts. They remain `PROPOSED_NOT_RUN`; source/AOI availability was not freshly verified, no numeric improvement is estimated, and none is a lane assignment. Their mechanism, implementation differences, cost, mimics, and official/free-source limitations are retained in [`registry/focus9_novel_hypotheses_20261028.json`](../registry/focus9_novel_hypotheses_20261028.json).

1. **Pleistocene Lake Lahontan shoreline displacement:** use raw USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM tiles and age/level-tagged USGS NGMDB shoreline geometry, with native elevation/slope and independent subsurface context. Test repeated offsets/tilts of the same-age shoreline bench—not generic elevation edges. Potentially reveals a concealed fault that displaced a former lake-level datum. High implementation cost. Mimics include wave-cut benches, shoreline facies, compaction, terraces, and volcanic margins. Existing scarp/knickpoint methods treat shorelines as masks/mimics, not as a positive age-matched displacement datum. Official source catalogs: [USGS 3DEP](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program), [USGS National Geologic Map Database](https://ngmdb.usgs.gov/). Exact AOI tiles, map scale, shoreline age geometry, and current availability were not verified; raw inputs are absent.
2. **Time-ordered ComCat sequence migration:** obtain USGS ComCat event time, hypocenter, magnitude, event type, and location uncertainty; fit repeated migrating sequences along a buried plane and test completeness/declustering controls. This differs from existing static event-density/count-band features. Very high cost. Mimics include geothermal injection/production, blasts, mining, volcanic swarms, known-fault aftershocks, and network-detection changes. Official endpoint: [USGS ComCat FDSN Event Service](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/). No event time series is local; the endpoint was not queried.
3. **Age-tagged stratigraphic-contact offset graph:** use attributed unit polygons from a specific USGS/state geologic map plus native depth-to-basement, RTP, gravity, and conductivity context; identify several age/lithology-matched contacts that terminate or step coherently across a corridor. Existing source methods use fault-proxy rasters and potential-field edges, not age-aware contact correspondence and displacement. High implementation cost. Mimics include intrusive/volcanic contacts, depositional/alluvial boundaries, map-sheet joins, generalized lines, age errors, and georeferencing mismatch. Official catalog: [USGS National Geologic Map Database](https://ngmdb.usgs.gov/). Exact product, vector polygons, scale, metadata, and AOI coverage were not verified; the local derived fault raster is not a substitute.

The availability statements are intentionally conservative: these are official/free-source candidates, not sources verified as downloadable or usable in this sandbox. Direct USGS/ComCat hosts are outside the current egress allowlist. The hypotheses may be reconsidered only after the literal gate is resolved under an authorized protocol and the required input/cache is restored.
